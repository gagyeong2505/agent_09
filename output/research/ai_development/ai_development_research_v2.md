# AI Development — Research Findings (v2)

- Topic: The development of Artificial Intelligence
- Research date: 2026-10-04
- Method: Web research (WebSearch / WebFetch); figures are quoted from the sources listed at the end

---

## 1. Historical Background

| Period | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1956 | Dartmouth Workshop — the term "Artificial Intelligence" is coined |
| 1970s–1990s | Symbolic AI and expert systems; two "AI winters" caused by unmet expectations |
| 1997 | IBM Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov |
| 2012 | AlexNet wins ImageNet — the start of the deep learning era |
| 2016 | DeepMind AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol in Go |
| 2017 | "Attention Is All You Need" introduces the Transformer architecture |
| 2022 | ChatGPT launches; generative AI reaches the mass market |
| 2024–2026 | Multimodal models, reasoning models and AI agents become mainstream |

## 2. Current State of Technology (Stanford AI Index 2026)

- **Capability is accelerating, not plateauing.** Performance on SWE-bench Verified (software engineering) jumped from about 60% to nearly 100% in a single year.
- Frontier models meet or exceed human baselines on PhD-level science questions, competition mathematics and multimodal reasoning. Gemini Deep Think won a gold medal at the International Mathematical Olympiad.
- **The "jagged frontier":** the same top models read analog clocks correctly only 50.1% of the time. Capability is uneven across tasks.
- **Industry dominance:** industry produced over 90% of notable frontier models in 2025.
- **US–China gap has closed:** US and Chinese models have traded the lead several times since early 2025. As of March 2026 the top US model (Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6) led the best Chinese model by only 2.7%.

## 3. Adoption and Investment

- Organizational AI adoption reached **88%** in 2025 (up from 78% the year before). 79% of respondents report active generative AI use in at least one business function.
- Generative AI reached **53% of the population within three years**, faster than the personal computer or the internet.
- Four in five university students use generative AI.
- **Global corporate AI investment reached $581.7 billion.** US private AI investment was $285.9 billion in 2025, more than 23 times China's $12.4 billion.
- The estimated value of generative AI tools to US consumers reached **$172 billion per year** by early 2026.
- Companies allocate up to 20% of their technology budget to AI; 58% of companies increased AI investment in 2026.

## 4. The Rise of AI Agents

- 62% of organizations are at least experimenting with AI agents: 23% are scaling agentic AI in at least one business function and 39% are experimenting.
- 97% of executives say their company deployed AI agents in the past year, and 52% of employees already use them (WRITER survey).
- **Adoption friction is growing:** 79% of organizations report challenges adopting AI, a double-digit increase from 2025.
- McKinsey's 2026 Technology Trends Outlook adds two new domains: *agentic software development* and *AI for scientific discovery and engineering*.

## 5. Infrastructure, Compute and Energy

- The US hosts **5,427 data centers**, more than 10 times any other country. TSMC fabricates nearly all leading AI chips, which concentrates supply-chain risk.
- In 2025, data-center electricity demand rose 17% and **AI-focused data-center demand rose 50%**, against 3% growth in global electricity demand (IEA).
- The IEA projects data-center electricity use will roughly double from **485 TWh (2025) to 950 TWh (2030)**, about 3% of global demand. AI-focused data-center consumption is expected to triple.
- Capital expenditure of the largest technology companies exceeded $400 billion in 2025 and is expected to rise by a further 75% in 2026.
- Energy per AI task is falling by at least an order of magnitude per year, but growing usage outweighs these efficiency gains.

## 6. Impact on Work and Society

- AI skills appear in 2.5% of all US job postings, up 55% year over year (AI Index 2026).
- WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025: by 2030, **170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced**, a net gain of 78 million. This figure covers overall labor-market change, not AI alone, and displaced workers do not move into new roles without reskilling.
- AI and information processing are expected to transform 86% of businesses by 2030.
- **Perception gap:** 73% of experts expect AI to have a positive effect on jobs, compared with 23% of the public, a 50-point gap.
- AI talent moving to the US has fallen 89% since 2017.

## 7. Safety, Ethics and Regulation

- Documented AI incidents rose from **233 (2024) to 362 (2025)**. Safety benchmark reporting is still inconsistent across developers.
- Only about half of US middle and high schools have AI policies.
- **EU AI Act:**
  - Obligations for general-purpose AI (GPAI) model providers have applied since 2 August 2025. They cover technical documentation, information for downstream providers, a training-data summary, a copyright policy, and, for systemic-risk models, adversarial testing and incident reporting.
  - Most remaining provisions apply from 2 August 2026, although the European Commission has proposed postponing some high-risk obligations.
  - Fines for GPAI non-compliance can reach €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. More than 20 providers have signed the GPAI Code of Practice.
- **Korea's AI Basic Act** (Framework Act on AI Development and Establishment of Trust):
  - Took effect on **22 January 2026**, making Korea the second jurisdiction after the EU with a comprehensive AI law.
  - Key duties: transparency (including labeling of AI-generated content), safety measures, special obligations for operators of high-impact AI, AI impact assessments, and appointing a domestic representative for foreign operators.
  - The law is framed as promotion first. Fines are deferred by a guidance period of at least one year.

## 8. Outlook

1. **From models to systems:** value is moving from standalone models to agents that plan, use tools and carry out multi-step work.
2. **AI for science:** AI-driven discovery in biology, materials and mathematics is becoming a distinct trend.
3. **Compute and energy as strategic assets:** power supply, chips and data centers increasingly limit how fast AI can grow.
4. **Governance catching up:** regulation is moving from principles to enforcement (EU, Korea), with AI sovereignty becoming a policy priority.
5. **Human–AI collaboration and reskilling:** the main labor-market question is shifting from replacement to how work is redesigned.

## 9. Key Takeaways for the Report

- AI capability keeps improving quickly, but unevenly (the "jagged frontier").
- Adoption is close to universal, while getting real value at scale (agents, ROI) is still hard.
- Energy, chips and talent are the new competitive bottlenecks.
- Safety and regulation are lagging but speeding up. 2026 is the first year that both the EU and Korean laws apply.
